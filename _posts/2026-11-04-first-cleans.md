Welcome to Sole Society. For our very first post we pulled two well-worn pairs
and did something simple: **clean one shoe, leave the other alone**, and put
them next to each other. No filters, no tricks. Just a brush, the right
cleaner and some patience.

## Pair 1: Navy &amp; White Jordan 1 Low

<span class="badge">Leather</span>

The toe boxes on these had gone from white to grey, and the midsoles were
carrying months of dirt. Left shoe is how it came in. Right shoe is after the
clean.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/posts/first-clean/navy-aj1-split.jpg" alt="Navy and white Jordan 1 Lows, left shoe dirty and right shoe cleaned" caption="Before (left) vs. after (right). Look at that toe box." %}

The leather came back bright and the navy panels got their depth back.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/posts/first-clean/navy-aj1-side.jpg" alt="Side profile of both navy Jordan 1 Lows" caption="Side by side. The top shoe's midsole is cleaned, the bottom one is not." %}

Outsoles matter too.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/images/posts/first-clean/navy-aj1-soles.jpg" alt="Outsoles of the navy Jordan 1 Lows" caption="Outsoles: left is before, right is after. Grime packs into those tread rings." %}

## Pair 2: Black &amp; Grey Nike Dunk Low

<span class="badge">Leather / Synthetic</span>

These are black and grey Dunks. Believe it or not, the left shoe is supposed to
be black too. Dirt and wear faded it to a dusty grey. Same deal as the Jordans:
right shoe cleaned, left shoe untouched.

## The drying station

Rushing the dry is how good shoes get ruined, so every pair finishes on the
**Sole Society Precision Air Drying System&trade;**. Twin towers. Gentle, even
airflow. Officially branded, as you can see.

Some people might call it a winter boot dryer. We call it proprietary
technology.
